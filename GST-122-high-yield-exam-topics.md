# GST 122 Repository Analysis

**Repository:** [dbolaji1/GST-122](https://github.com/dbolaji1/GST-122)  
**Purpose:** Identification of the five highest-yield examination topics from the repository’s lecture notes, PDFs, slide materials, summaries, case studies and revision resources.

## Method

The repository’s 18 PDF resources were reviewed, including the 118-page core course text, lecture-style slide PDFs, weekly summaries, revision materials, case studies, self-assessment sections and practice questions. Page numbers below refer to PDF pages. For PDFs converted from PowerPoint slides, the PDF page number is treated as the slide number.

## Study Priority

### 1. Writing skills and composition

This should receive the greatest amount of study time because it is the central organizing theme of the course. It is covered across the first five core study sessions and reinforced by several separate materials. It also contains the largest number of practical applications and assessment-style tasks.

**Repeated concepts:**

- Planning before writing
- Brainstorming and research
- Outlining
- Introduction, body and conclusion
- Editing and proofreading
- Clarity, coherence, unity and correctness
- Summary, letter, report and essay writing
- Narrative, descriptive, argumentative and persuasive writing

### 2. Grammar, sentence structure and mechanics

Grammar is highly examinable because it is distributed across several self-contained resources. Students should expect questions requiring identification, classification, correction or application rather than only definition recall.

**Repeated concepts:**

- Sentence structure and sentence types
- Parts of speech
- Function words and content words
- Concord/agreement
- Punctuation and capitalization
- Run-on sentences and fragments
- Correct word order and verb forms
- Syntax, clauses and grammatical functions

### 3. Speaking, pronunciation and public speaking

Speaking combines technical language knowledge with practical application. It is especially important because the repository contains both theoretical content—vowels, consonants, stress and intonation—and applied content involving speech preparation, delivery, audience awareness and response to prompts.

**Repeated concepts:**

- Active control of pronunciation
- Vowels, consonants and diphthongs
- Stress, intonation and accent
- Speech purpose and audience
- Informative, demonstrative and persuasive speeches
- Eye contact, gestures and body language
- Tone, vocal variety and voice control
- Anxiety management and rehearsal

### 4. Active listening, note-taking and comprehension

This topic is supported by multiple dedicated resources and practice sections. It is also explicitly connected to academic success, communication effectiveness and comprehension of complex material.

**Repeated concepts:**

- Hearing versus listening
- Active versus passive listening
- Identifying main ideas and supporting information
- Critical and empathetic listening
- Avoiding miscommunication
- Cornell note-taking
- Mind mapping
- SQ3R: Survey, Question, Read, Recite and Review
- Inference and interpretation

### 5. Argumentation, inductive and deductive reasoning

This ranks fifth because its coverage is narrower than general writing, grammar, speaking and listening, but it has unusually concentrated examination value: a dedicated reasoning resource, an argumentative-writing section, case-study references and explicit final-course emphasis.

**Repeated concepts:**

- Premises and conclusions
- Inductive versus deductive reasoning
- Generalization and prediction
- Analogical and statistical reasoning
- Syllogisms
- Modus ponens and modus tollens
- Thesis and evidence
- Counterarguments
- Logical organization
- Source evaluation and critical thinking

## Most Repeated Cross-Repository Ideas

The following concepts recur across the greatest number of resources and should be treated as cross-topic priorities:

1. **Organization:** outlines, introductions, bodies, conclusions and logical sequencing.
2. **Clarity and correctness:** grammar, punctuation, appropriate vocabulary, editing and proofreading.
3. **Active engagement:** active listening, reading strategies, questioning, recitation and revision.
4. **Audience awareness:** adapting writing and speech to purpose, context and audience.
5. **Evidence and support:** supporting sentences, examples, facts, reasons, evidence and counterarguments.
6. **Critical thinking:** inference, evaluating information, reasoning, persuasive communication and source evaluation.
